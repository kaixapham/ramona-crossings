(03) The slow way

We still cross the bay the slow way, by steam.

No diesel. No hurry.

In a harbour of outboards and water taxis, we choose the long way round. A coal fire, a brass whistle and a crossing that takes as long as it takes, which is ninety minutes, there and back.

A good crossing is mostly patience, and a little coal.

(Wind) North-west, most evenings. It drops at sunset and the bay goes flat as a plate.

(Speed) Seven knots, never more. The wake stays small enough to rock the moored dinghies, not to swamp them.

(Seats) Twenty-four, on teak benches. Nobody stands, and nobody is more than two metres from the rail.

(06) The route

Old Quay to Lighthouse Point.

6.4 nautical miles, there and back. Whatever her speed, the wake opens behind her at 19.47°.

A small crew, a few regulars, and whoever the evening brings aboard.

Skipper, stoker, a deckhand who knows every buoy by name, and a kettle that has never been allowed to go cold.

Leaveonly awake.

She burns seasoned beech offcuts from the boatyard next door. The ash goes to the gardens on the quay, the bilge is pumped ashore, and the only thing she leaves on the bay is a V of white water that closes behind her in a minute.
